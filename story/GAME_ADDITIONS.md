## Community holiday traditions

### Community Thanksgiving Gathering

Fourth Thursday in November · Town Square.

Thanksgiving in Honeybrook is gratitude to God, remembrance, family, community and care for neighbors. A shared harvest meal is one part of a deeper gathering; everyone has a place at the table.

The community pauses to give thanks to God for life, provision, family and the neighbors who carry one another through difficult seasons. Loved ones are remembered, Honeybrook’s beginnings are honored, and gratitude becomes action: a place at the table, a shared portion and a visit for someone who cannot come. The feast is an expression of that care.

- **Gather in gratitude and remembrance**: Prepare a quiet reflection corner; Welcome neighbors to a voluntary blessing or quiet reflection; Pause to remember loved ones and Honeybrook’s beginnings; Arrange portions and visits for neighbors unable to attend.
- **Set the Town Square tables**: Lay the harvest tablecloths; Place the plates and cups; Leave wide clear aisles and family spaces; Arrange a quiet nearby table.
- **Cook the harvest supper**: Measure the ingredients; Mix the bowl; Bake / simmer the holiday batch; Inspect and label the finished dish.
- **Make the community gratitude tree**: Write a gratitude leaf: blessings, family, remembrance or a neighbor you want to thank..
- **Lead the harvest melody**: Play the community melody at an unhurried pace.
- **Serve every community place**: Welcome each registered community place with a portion or non-food favor.
- **Leave the square welcoming**: Gather reusable tableware; Pack the remaining portions for sharing; Wipe the tables and clear the paths; Return the kitchen and square supplies.

Finale: Open the community Thanksgiving dinner. Keepsake: Harvest table quilt square.

The Christmas Jubilee honors the birth of Jesus Christ alongside Santa Claus traditions, joyful giving, carols and the lighting of the community tree. Sacred remembrance and playful Christmas wonder both belong here.

Families are checked in and assigned together; Lena participates with her family through supported seating, transfer and quiet-space preparation. Visiting parties can be registered. ### Nell

The familiar stranger · investigator from a lineage of beekeepers. Nell’s golden hair is the first resemblance the bears see. As a private investigator, she compares records, separates belief from evidence and listens before drawing conclusions. In her spare time she keeps bees. She is part of a long family lineage of beekeepers; almost everyone in the family loves bees. Her own thorough knowledge belongs within that shared tradition. Her patient care, loving attention, curious questions and lifelong ease around bears make the resemblance feel deeper. She is drawn to Honeybrook before she understands the connection; she wants to know its paths, homes and people before seeking Harold. The bears’ belief surrounds her arrival, while her grandfather’s promise and the goddess’s dream remain her own story to share. Nell is Honeybrook’s first present-day human arrival. She comes to discover the place she heard about as a child; her welcome is never something she must buy. Private investigator; she keeps bees in her spare time. Nell belongs to a long family line of beekeepers. Almost everyone in her family loves bees, and she knows their shared trade thoroughly. Nell has loved bears for as long as she can remember. She wonders whether that instinctive fondness has always drawn her toward this place. Before they know who Nell is, bears across Honeybrook and Bear Hollow immediately welcome her. Her golden hair, curious and loving nature, beekeeper’s care and instinctive affection for bears remind them so strongly of the beloved Goldilocks that they feel her spirit in Nell. Some believe Nell is Goldilocks reincarnated. This is their belief, not a confirmed answer to Nell’s identity. Keep the promise to her grandfather: visit Honeybrook and learn what happened to the beekeepers. Follow the goddess’s dream instruction to ask for Harold. Goldilocks’s mother was the daughter of the owner of oil-rich land. The same landowner is Nell’s great-great-great-grandfather. Her late grandfather left her an $800 trillion inheritance; her visit is about family history and the promise, not claiming a fortune.

- routine: Explore Honeybrook and Bear Hollow first. Beekeeping is the family business. The bears embrace a familiar-looking stranger before they know her identity; Nell chooses when she is ready to speak to Harold.

### Goldilocks’s special game letter · adaptation draft

To the family who came looking,

I do not know your name, or how many years this letter has waited. But if Harold has put it into your hands, then someone in our family still remembers us. Before anything else, please know this: the little girl lived.

My mother and father were lost in the river. I wish there were a gentler truth to give you. I wish I could write that I found them waiting on the other bank. I cannot. I carried what my mother had given me—a satchel, a jar of honey, and the blue-flowered cloth—and for a while I did not know where I belonged in the world.

Then a family of bears made a place for me. There was porridge, a chair, a bed. There were ordinary mornings after days when I had thought there could be no more ordinary mornings. Their home became my home. They did not replace my parents. They loved the child my parents had loved.

If you have spent years wondering whether I was frightened, I was, at first. If you have wondered whether I was alone, please let that worry rest now. I was not left alone in the woods. I grew older among those who knew my name and made room for me. There was grief here, but there was laughter too. There was a life beyond the river.

I wondered about you. I wondered whether someone still spoke my mother's name, whether a place at a family table had remained empty because no one knew what to do with it. I hoped someone would come. I could not know what you had heard, where you had searched, or what had kept our paths apart.

So I asked Harold to keep this letter. If any human ever came to Honeybrook or Bear Hollow asking about the beekeepers, he was to give it to them. I wanted the answer to be waiting, even if I could no longer answer the door myself.

Please do not carry this home as another reason to punish yourselves. Tell them what you have learned. Tell them that their little girl was loved. Tell them that loving the family I found never meant forgetting the family I came from.

And if someone who searched for us is no longer there to hear you, speak their name when you tell the story. Let them have a place in the answer they waited for. I cannot give back the years. I can leave you these words: I lived. I remembered. You were never strangers to my heart.

With all my love,
Goldilocks

## Nell’s discovery scene

Nell lowered the letter, but she could not loosen her fingers from its edges. For a while she could see very little of the room. She kept thinking of a child watching a path, and of a family searching from the other side of the woods.

They might have come closer than any of them ever knew.

Perhaps they had stood close enough to hear the bees. Perhaps someone had called her name, waited, and called again. Nell imagined a searcher noticing bear tracks in the earth, looking towards the thicker trees and thinking of the people who still had to be brought safely home.

She could imagine the frightened decision to turn back. They had already lost people they loved. Going farther might have seemed like risking another loss.

There was no record that could tell Nell exactly where they had stood, or how near their voices might have carried. She could not make a certainty out of something she wished so desperately had ended differently. But the possibility hurt enough.

Somewhere beyond the place where they stopped, a little girl might have been watching another path, waiting for a familiar face.

The family had wanted to find her. Goldilocks had wanted to be found. There had been love on both sides of the trees, and neither side had known how to reach the other.

Nell pressed her lips together. It did not keep her from crying.

They had feared the very creatures keeping her safe.

To the searchers, bear tracks had meant danger. To Goldilocks, the bears had meant food, a bed, and someone who came when she woke frightened. The place her family had been afraid to enter had become her home.

Nell thought of the years that had followed: birthdays her relatives never saw, ordinary mornings they could not picture, the familiar voices Goldilocks had carried in her memory as she grew older. She thought of her grandfather asking his old question, never knowing that there was an answer waiting in a bear’s care.

If only they had known where to ask.

The words came to her again and again. Each time, they opened the same impossible wish. She wanted to go back to the woodland boundary, take those frightened people by the hand and show them the way. She wanted to tell the child to keep watching just a little longer.

She could do neither.

Harold set a glass of water within reach and sat quietly beside her. Nell looked at his paw resting near the letter. She remembered the welcomes she had received since arriving, before anyone knew whose family she belonged to. No one had asked her to earn a place at the table.

Goldilocks had known that kindness too. For years. For a whole life.

Nell read the letter again, more slowly. This time she tried to make room for the life it described as well as the years her family had missed. There had been meals shared, work learned, friends loved and days worth waking for. Goldilocks had been mourned here because she had belonged here.

A small laugh escaped Nell when she remembered how warmly the bears had fussed over her own arrival. It broke into another sob, and she wiped her face with an impatient hand. She could almost understand how a frightened child might gradually have learned to laugh in this place.

She wished her family could see it. She wanted them to understand that the bears had loved the little girl they were searching for.

Nell reached for her notebook. She would have to tell them about the river and the waiting. She would have to tell them why she was crying. But she would tell them about the home beyond the trees as well, and the kindness that had filled it.

For generations, the family had been left with the day Goldilocks disappeared. Now Nell could bring them the rest of her life.

## Weather and essential access

Essential refuge, care, ordinary welcome, public paths and belonging remain free. Paid trading work pauses during a simulated storm. The global warning retains its 30-second shelter route prompt. The fictional storm ends after three minutes of real elapsed time, clears the red banner and siren, and displays an eight-second all-clear. Its clock survives navigation and reload. Original shelter check-ins and unfinished work are retained; quiet town-refuge readiness practice can continue afterward without restarting the alarm. Repeated camp warnings have unique incident identifiers. Manual full-readiness all-clear remains available sooner when its actual checks are complete. This is game weather, not real emergency guidance.
