# Nell's first-person story scenes

Four original landscape photographic-style scenes generated with the built-in ImageGen tool, October 23, 2026. Reviewed individually, then converted from PNG to WebP at quality 88. The manifest records final dimensions and SHA-256 values. No prior source image was overwritten.

Prompt set: richly textured realistic Honeybrook woodland bear-country photography, stone/timber architecture, honey/pine palette, believable furnishings, restrained practical light, no captions or logos. All four use the player's first-person view and show no invented human face, hands, body or reflection.

- `arrival.webp`: rental car with a modest dent, camera/notebook/travel bag, Welcome House, repaired wooden creek bridge and distant bears.
- `room.webp`: furnished Welcome House spare bedroom, bed, writing desk, notebook/camera, window toward creek/pines, warm modest lamps.
- `letter.webp`: Harold as a kindly elderly mail bear across the post-office desk, satchel, ordinary correspondence and a honeycomb-sealed envelope; no readable in-image text. The interactive letter text is rendered separately and opens only on the player's deliberate action.
- `night.webp`: upstairs north-facing window, wooden creek bridge, uphill pine path, moon and stars, a few distant lights, notebook/letters and Sammy's honey bun. No embodied goddess, invented monster or resolved woods mystery.

Nell's likeness has not been inferred. Her age, marriage, family, inheritance and promise are recorded in `nell-data.js` and `story/NELL_GAME_CANON.md`; the original manuscript remains unchanged. The earlier dream is described in the player's remembered opening and does not claim that a goddess's physical appearance has been revealed.

The fifth asset, `nell.webp`, is an original fictional portrait of golden-haired Nell as a 42-year-old British human woman, with a notebook and camera satchel. Its invented appearance is a game design, not a verified likeness of the author. The transparent portrait is used for Nell’s profile and player figure; the four environment photographs retain their first-person composition.
